Illustrative rows only, drawn with a >= 20 so "% serious" is a meaningful fraction. The cohort threshold is unchanged (T1: a >= 3).

Y = 1 means the event was ABSENT from the drug's label at the detection quarter and a safety labelling change named it within the next 36 months. The baseline check (decisions 74-78, 99-101) reads the DailyMed label version in force at detection; pairs whose label already carried the event are dropped from the cohort rather than scored, so no row here is a restatement of something already known. Applying that rule cut T1 from 349,242 rows and 3,912 positives to 241,443 and 966 (1.12% -> 0.400%): 69.7% of the old positives were events the label already carried.

Two known limits of the baseline check, both measured: its hand-checked accuracy is 28/30 on exclusions with no miss found in 30 kept positives, and where a drug's primary registration has no usable history the baseline falls back to another set id for that ingredient, which is a median 6.9 years stale.
